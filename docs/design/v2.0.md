# Sleuthline 2.0 — design (draft for review)

Status: **draft**, to be reviewed before any code. Builds on 1.2 (several hosts per case).

Goal of 2.0: replace the fixed list of playbook steps with a **decision graph**. What the agent finds decides what is checked next, the links between pieces of evidence are computed by the program, and every important conclusion is tested against what could contradict it. Three parts, delivered one after the other, each useful on its own:

1. **Entity graph** — the program links every file, hash, address, account, process and host to the records where it appears (no model call).
2. **Leads and investigation tasks** — a short triage pass produces leads; a scheduler turns them into focused tasks, following decision rules; each task can open new leads.
3. **Hypotheses and counter-evidence** — the attack is described as hypotheses with what supports them, what contradicts them and what is still open.

Inputs: the suggestion received after 1.0 ("a decision graph"), the 1.0–1.2 runs on Case 001, the external reviews, the 1.1 lesson: **skipping a step because nothing "should" be there loses findings** (on the memory-only and network-only cases, almost every step found something). 2.0 therefore never skips on a guess: tasks are added on evidence, steps are only skipped when their data is absent (as in 1.1).

Out of scope: daylight saving time (needs a European collection), Linux hosts, per-step model choice (separate, small change; it combines with 2.0).

## 1. Entity graph (2.0a)

### Today
Pivots are made by the agent, one SQL query at a time: find a hash, search it elsewhere, search the IP of that flow on the other host… The links exist only in the conversation and in the text of the findings.

### 2.0a
`normalize.py` (and `merge.py` for a multi-host case) builds two tables:

| table | content |
|---|---|
| `entities` | `entity_id`, `type` (file, hash, ip, domain, url, account, sid, process, service, task, registry_key, host), `value` (as seen), `norm` (normalized: lower case, `C:\Windows\System32\X.exe` and `\Device\HarddiskVolume3\Windows\System32\x.exe` → same file, IPv4 without leading zeros, SID as is), `first_ts`, `last_ts`, `hosts`, `refs_count` |
| `entity_refs` | `entity_id`, `ref`, `ts`, `host_id`, `artifact`, `role` (e.g. `image`, `parent`, `target`, `src_ip`, `dst_ip`, `account`, `hash_of`) |

- **Extraction, deterministic**, from mapped columns first (`path`, `user_name`, `host` of the timeline; hash columns already found by `hash_hits`; Suricata `src_ip` / `dest_ip` / `http_hostname` / `tls_sni` / `fileinfo_sha256`; Volatility `ImageFileName`, `PID` / `PPID`, `ForeignAddr`; services and tasks from their raw tables), then regular expressions on `description` for IPs, hashes, URLs and domains. Each extractor is declared in `normalize_map.json` next to the source it reads (same circuit as the mappings: validated in the repository).
- **Co-occurrence edges are not stored**: they are computed on demand (same record, or same host within a time window), so the database does not explode.
- **Multi-host**: entities are shared across hosts by `norm`; the same SHA-256 on two hosts is one entity with refs on both — the cross-host link is free.
- **Size**: on Case 001 full (847 k timeline rows) an estimated 1–3 M `entity_refs` rows; noise entities (`C:\Windows\System32\svchost.exe`, `127.0.0.1`, `SYSTEM`) are kept but flagged `common` (seen on > N % of the records of their artifact) so that pivots skip them by default.

New agent tools (read-only, results wrapped as untrusted evidence like every data tool):
- `pivot(value or entity_id, host?, around?)`: every record where the entity appears, grouped by host and artifact, with times, plus the entities seen in the same records;
- `neighbours(entity_id, window_s, host?)`: entities active on the same host within ± window around its records, ranked by rarity.

Report: an **attack graph** figure built from the findings of severity ≥ medium: their entities (files, hashes, IPs, accounts) and hosts, with the edges given by the shared records. Static picture (no model), embedded in section 3.

Cost: **no model call**; normalization time +10–20 % (to be measured).

## 2. Leads and investigation tasks (2.0b)

### Today
14 steps in a fixed order, each with a budget of 40 tool calls, whatever was found before. Following a lead found in step 3 waits for the step whose topic it is, or never happens.

### 2.0b: triage, then tasks driven by leads

```
                      ┌────────────── triage (steps 1-12 of today, shorter) ──────────────┐
evidence of the case ─┤  each step records findings AND leads (add_lead)                 ├─► lead queue
                      └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘        │
                                                                                                    ▼
                         decision rules (program) ──► tasks (focused conversations) ──► findings, new leads
                                                                                                    │
                                                         stops when: no lead above the threshold,  │
                                                         depth reached, or task budget spent ◄──────┘
                                                                     │
                                         correlation (step 13) ──► hypotheses (2.0c) ──► summary (step 14)
```

- **Triage**: the 12 topic steps of today keep running (no skip on a guess), with a smaller budget (default 20 tool calls instead of 40). Their job changes: find what is there, record findings, and record **leads** for what deserves a deeper look, instead of trying to finish every thread.
- **Lead** = a question about the case, with its seeds: `add_lead(question, entities, reason, refs, priority)`. Examples: *"Where did the RDP session to DESKTOP-SDN1RPT from 10.42.85.10 come from?"* (seed: ip 10.42.85.10, account C137\Administrator), *"Was lsass memory read by the injected spoolsv.exe?"*.
- **Decision rules** (`config\decision_rules.json`, versioned and editable like the playbook) open leads **by the program**, from what was recorded. Each rule: a condition on findings / entities / detections, the question template, the seeds, a priority. First set (about 15 rules), for instance:

  | when | lead opened |
  |---|---|
  | logon type 10 / 3 from an internal IP | find the source host; outbound RDP / SMB / WinRM there; same account elsewhere |
  | logon from an external IP | brute force before it; other accounts from that IP |
  | new service or Run key pointing to a file | origin of the file (download, copy, archive), its hash on every host, its executions |
  | executable with network activity (memory, SRUM, capture) | the remote addresses on every host and in the capture; the same hash elsewhere |
  | process with a handle to `lsass.exe`, `vol_malfind` hit in a privileged process | credential access: dump files, `sekurlsa`-like strings, later logons with the accounts present on that host |
  | IOC recorded (hash, IP, domain, file name) | sweep of the IOC on every host and in the capture (program, then a task only if there are hits outside the known findings) |
  | archive created then deleted, large outbound flow | exfiltration channel: C2 sessions, cloud uploads, removable media |
  | anti-forensics signal (log cleared, timestomp SI<FN, Defender disabled) | what the attacker was hiding: activity in the window before |
  | question of the guidance file not answered | a task dedicated to it |

- **Scheduler** (program): deduplicates leads (same entities and question), orders them by priority (severity of the source finding, rule priority, rarity of the entities), and runs each one as a **task**: a short conversation (default 15 tool calls) that receives the question, the seeds already resolved with `pivot`, the relevant findings, and the host scope. A task ends with `task_complete(outcome, findings, new leads)`: *confirmed / refuted / inconclusive* and why.
- **Bounds** (configurable, defaults): depth 3 (a lead opened by a task opened by a task…), 25 tasks per case, priority threshold, plus the existing `max_billed_tokens_per_run`. When a bound stops the queue, the remaining leads are listed in the report as *not investigated*, never silently dropped.
- **Multi-host**: a lead names its host(s); tasks on one host run with the host scope of 1.2, tasks across hosts run at case level.
- **Decision trace**: every lead keeps who opened it (rule or agent, from which finding), its task, its outcome and what it produced. This is the "why" of the analysis.

Report: new appendix *Decision trace* (lead → task → outcome → findings), and in the limitations the leads not investigated and why (budget, depth, threshold).

## 3. Hypotheses and counter-evidence (2.0c)

### Today
A finding has a confidence computed from the number of independent sources. Nothing asks what would contradict it, and the attack story lives only in the summary text.

### 2.0c
- New table `hypotheses` (`id`, `statement`, `tactic` / phase, `hosts`, `status`: supported / refuted / open, `confidence`), and links `finding → hypothesis` with a relation: **supports**, **contradicts**, **context**.
- The correlation step builds the hypotheses from the findings (*"initial access to DESKTOP-SDN1RPT by RDP from the DC with the domain Administrator account"*). For each hypothesis of a critical or high finding, the scheduler opens a **challenge lead**: *what would contradict it, and is it there?* (e.g. another logon earlier, the account used legitimately at that time, the file present before the incident window, a clock offset that reverses the order). The task records what it checked, found or not.
- **Retained confidence** of a hypothesis (enforced by the program, like the evidence policy): `confirmed` only with ≥ 2 independent supporting sources **and** no unresolved contradiction **and** its challenge task run; `probable` otherwise; `refuted` if a contradiction is confirmed. The rules are in `evidence_policy`, configurable.
- Report: the executive summary and the timeline are built from the supported hypotheses; a table *Hypotheses* (statement, status, supporting / contradicting evidence, what was checked) in section 3; refuted hypotheses are kept and shown (they prove the alternatives were tested).

## Cost

Reference: Case 001 full, single host, 1.0: 11.76 USD, 14 steps, 237 model calls. Estimates, to be measured:

| part | effect | estimate |
|---|---|---|
| 2.0a entity graph | no model call; fewer exploratory queries (pivots served ready) | −5 to −15 % |
| 2.0b triage + tasks | shorter steps; tasks only where leads exist. Quiet case: big saving. Rich case like Case 001: most leads are worth following | −10 to +10 % on Case 001; −30 to −50 % on a quiet case |
| 2.0c challenges | one challenge task per critical / high hypothesis | +15 to +30 % |
| **total** | | **about the same on Case 001 (± 20 %)**, lower on quiet cases; bounded by the task budget |

The gain of 2.0 is quality and explainability (leads followed to the end, alternatives tested, the "why" of each check), not cost. The cost lever stays the per-step model choice, which can come before or after.

## Tests

- 2.0a: extraction on the synthetic cases (paths normalized, hashes, IPs from description and from Suricata, Volatility processes), shared entity across two hosts, `common` flag, `pivot` / `neighbours` output, figure generated.
- 2.0b: rules on recorded findings open the expected leads; dedup; priority order; bounds (depth, tasks, budget) stop the queue and list the rest; a task ends with an outcome; mock provider: call count per task; decision trace in the report.
- 2.0c: hypothesis with support only → probable until its challenge task ran; a confirmed contradiction → refuted; the policy is enforced whatever the model declares.
- The 1.2 tests keep passing; `--classic` keeps the 1.2 flow (fixed steps) for comparison.

## Validation

Case 001 full (single host) and two hosts (workstation + DC + capture), with the 1.2 run as reference: **no finding of 1.2 lost**, findings added by the tasks, leads investigated / not investigated, cost and duration. A second, quieter case (e.g. Threat Hunting Lab 1) to measure the saving on a less compromised machine.

## Delivery

4. README, CHANGELOG `2.0.0` after the validation runs.

## Open questions

1. **Triage budget**: 20 tool calls per topic step instead of 40 — enough, given that the tasks then go deep? (On the 1.0 full run, the topic steps used 17 to 45 tool calls, median 29; step 4, memory, hit the limit.)
2. **Task bounds**: 25 tasks per case and depth 3 by default — or a budget in USD per case, converted to tokens?
3. **Rules**: start with the ~15 rules of the table (Windows intrusion), editable in `decision_rules.json` — any rule you see missing from your own cases?
4. **Order of delivery**: 2.0a alone first (free, visible in the report), or 2.0a + 2.0b together?
